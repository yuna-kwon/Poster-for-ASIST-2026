# Poster-for-ASIST-2026
This repository includes the paper for "Historians’ Information Practices with Social Media Data and Implications for Archiving", accepted for the poster session at the 89th Annual Meeting of the Association for Information Science and Technology (2026 ASIS&amp;T Annual Meeting).


## ABSTRACT
Acknowledging the importance of social media data as emerging primary sources for understanding the culture and history of the contemporary era, many scholars have emphasized the need to collect and preserve this data as digital heritage for future access and use. However, existing research on social media archiving has primarily focused on storing and organizing information, while paying relatively little attention to the actual use of archived data. To inform more user-centered archival approaches that enhance accessibility and effective use, this study examines historians’ information practices when engaging with archived social media data. Drawing on Yakel and Torres’s archival intelligence, Ellis’s information-seeking behavior model, and Kuhlthau’s information search process, this study adopts a mixed-methods approach, using questionnaires, semi-structured interviews, and non-participant observation. The study aims to contribute to both scholarship and practice by examining historians’ information needs and challenges and identifying implications for the design of user-centered archival systems.
